Recruiters search their applicant tracking system for specific skills, tools and job titles, then skim the resumes that come back. If the words they search for are not on your resume, you can be a strong candidate and still not appear. Keywords are how your experience gets found.

The good news: you do not need a list of "magic" resume words. The keywords that matter are already written in the job description.

## What counts as a keyword

Resume keywords are the specific terms an employer uses to describe the role. They usually fall into five groups:

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Hard skills and methods | financial modeling, A/B testing, SQL, user research |
| Tools and platforms | Salesforce, Figma, AWS, Excel, Jira |
| Job titles and seniority | product manager, senior accountant, team lead |
| Certifications and degrees | PMP, CPA, AWS Certified Solutions Architect, MBA |
| Domain terms | B2B SaaS, GAAP, HIPAA, supply chain |

Soft skills such as "communication" or "leadership" matter, but recruiters rarely search for them. Show them through results ("led a team of six") rather than listing them.

## How to find them in five minutes

1. **Paste the job description into a document.** Delete the company boilerplate and benefits section so you only see the role.
2. **Highlight every skill, tool, title and qualification.** Do not judge yet; just mark them.
3. **Look for repetition and position.** Terms in the job title, the first few bullets, or mentioned more than once are usually the priorities.
4. **Separate "required" from "nice to have".** Required items are the ones a recruiter is most likely to filter on.
5. **Compare two or three similar postings.** Terms that appear across several companies' listings for the same role are the industry's standard vocabulary, and they are worth having on your resume regardless.

You should end up with roughly 10 to 15 terms. Tick the ones you can honestly claim. Those are your keywords for this application.

## Where to put them

Placement matters as much as presence. Recruiters skim, and some ranking systems give more weight to skills that appear in context.

- **Summary.** Two or three of your strongest matching keywords, in a sentence about what you do.
- **Experience bullets.** The most important place. Show each core keyword in action, with a result:
  > Automated month-end reconciliations in **Excel** and **Power BI**, cutting the **close process** from 8 days to 5.
- **Skills section.** A clean list of tools and methods, using the employer's spelling ("Microsoft Excel" or "Excel", whichever they use).
- **Job titles.** If your official title was unusual but the work matches the target role, you can add a clarifying descriptor: "Customer Champion (Customer Success Manager)". Only do this when it is accurate.

## Match the exact wording

Search is literal. If the posting says "project management" and your resume only says "managed projects", a keyword search may miss you. A few habits help:

- **Use the employer's form of the term** when it accurately describes your work.
- **Include both the acronym and the full term once:** "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)".
- **Keep the version they use for plurals and variants:** "stakeholder management" versus "stakeholder relations".
- **Spell tools the official way:** "JavaScript", "PostgreSQL", "HubSpot".

## Avoid keyword stuffing

More is not better. Resumes that repeat the same term in every line, or paste the job description at the bottom in white text, are easy for recruiters to spot, and white text shows up plainly in the parsed version of your file.

A good rule: each core keyword should appear naturally once or twice, ideally in a bullet that shows a result and once in your skills list. If you cannot use a keyword in a true sentence about your experience, it does not belong on your resume.

## A quick worked example

Say a marketing coordinator posting mentions **HubSpot**, **email campaigns**, **A/B testing**, **content calendar** and **Google Analytics**.

**Before:**
> Helped with newsletters and social posts. Tracked results.

**After:**
> Planned and ran a weekly **content calendar** across email and social; built 30+ **email campaigns** in **HubSpot** and used **A/B testing** on subject lines to lift open rates from 21% to 27%, tracked in **Google Analytics**.

Same experience, now visible to anyone searching for any of those five skills.
