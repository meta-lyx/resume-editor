Almost every large employer now uses an applicant tracking system (ATS). In its 2026 ATS usage report, Jobscan detected one on the careers sites of [97.4% of Fortune 500 companies](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/fortune-500-use-applicant-tracking-systems/). Smaller companies increasingly use them too, often through job boards and HR platforms.

That makes it worth understanding what these systems do with your resume, and what they do not.

## What an ATS actually does

An ATS is the database a company uses to manage applicants. When you apply, it typically:

1. **Parses your resume**, pulling text into fields such as name, contact details, job titles, employers, dates, education and skills.
2. **Stores your application** so recruiters can review it, move it through stages and leave notes.
3. **Lets recruiters search and filter** candidates by keywords, titles, locations and answers to application questions.
4. **In some setups, ranks or scores** applications against the job description.

Applications can be rejected automatically by "knockout" questions, such as whether you are authorized to work in the country or hold a required license. Beyond those, a person usually decides who moves forward. The practical risk is less that a robot rejects you and more that your resume is parsed badly, so you do not show up when a recruiter searches for the skills you have.

### About the "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS" claim

You will see this number everywhere. It traces back to a job-services company called Preptel, which went out of business in 2013, and no published study supports it ([HiringThing](https://www.hiringthing.com/blog/applicant-tracking-system-myths) has a good write-up). Treat it as marketing, not data. The real goal is simpler: make your resume easy to parse and easy to find.

## Formatting choices that help

**Use standard section headings.** "Experience" (or "Work Experience"), "Education", "Skills", "Certifications". Creative headings like "Where I've Made an Impact" can confuse parsers that look for familiar labels.

**Keep one main column.** Many parsers read left to right, top to bottom. A two-column layout can interleave lines from both columns, scrambling your job titles and dates. If you love a sidebar, keep only non-essential items there and test the result (see below).

**Put key information in the body.** Some systems skip headers and footers. Your name, email and phone number belong at the top of the page itself.

**Avoid tables, text boxes and graphics for content.** Skill bars, icons, charts and photos either disappear or come through as noise. Write "Python (advanced)" instead of showing four out of five dots.

**Use a common font.** Arial, Calibri, Garamond, Georgia, Helvetica or similar, at 10 to 12 points for body text.

**Be consistent with dates.** Pick one format, such as "Jan 2023 – Present" or "01/2023 – Present", and use it everywhere. Put dates on the same line as each job title.

**Write out acronyms once.** "Certified Public Accountant (CPA)" lets a search for either form find you.

## PDF or Word?

Most modern ATS read text-based PDFs well, and PDF preserves your layout on every device. Follow the application's instructions when they ask for a specific format, and use Word (.docx) if they do.

Never upload a scanned image of a resume, or a PDF exported as an image. There is no text for the system to read.

## The 30-second parsing test

You do not need special software to check whether your resume is readable:

1. Open your PDF and select all the text (Ctrl+A or Cmd+A).
2. Copy and paste it into a plain text editor such as Notepad or TextEdit.
3. Read it top to bottom.

If your name, job titles, employers and dates come through in a sensible order, a parser will most likely handle it too. If sections are jumbled, text is missing, or characters turn into symbols, simplify the layout and test again.

## Formatting checklist

- One main column, standard section headings
- Contact details in the body, not the header
- No tables, text boxes, icons or skill graphs holding real content
- Common font, 10 to 12 point body text
- Consistent dates on the same line as each role
- Acronyms spelled out once
- Text-based PDF (or .docx if requested), clearly named
- Passes the copy-paste test
