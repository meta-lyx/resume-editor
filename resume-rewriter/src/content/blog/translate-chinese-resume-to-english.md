---
title: How to Translate a Chinese Resume into English for US Employers
description: Word-for-word translation makes a strong Chinese resume look weak in English. How to translate school names, job titles, awards and bullets so US recruiters understand them.
date: 2026-11-15
---

Translating a Chinese resume (中文简历) into English is not a language task. It is a rewriting task. A word-for-word translation keeps the Chinese structure, the Chinese conventions and the Chinese way of describing work, and a US recruiter reads it as unclear or junior.

The goal is a resume that reads as if it had been written in English from the start, with every fact still accurate.

## Step 1: Remove what US resumes leave out

Chinese resumes often include personal details that US employers deliberately avoid, because of anti-discrimination law. Delete these before you translate a word:

- 照片 (photo)
- 出生日期 / 年龄 (birth date, age)
- 性别 (gender)
- 民族 (ethnicity)
- 籍贯 / 户口 (hometown, household registration)
- 政治面貌 (political status, including 党员 or 共青团员)
- 婚姻状况 (marital status)
- 身高 / 体重 (height, weight)
- 身份证号 (ID number)

Also drop 求职意向 as a separate block and the 自我评价 paragraph of personal qualities ("吃苦耐劳, 团队精神强"). US resumes skip both. If you want a summary, write one or two lines about skills and goals, not character.

## Step 2: Translate names the official way

**Your name.** Use pinyin with given name first: 张伟 becomes **Wei Zhang**. If you use an English name, write **Wei (David) Zhang** or **David Zhang**, and match LinkedIn exactly.

**Universities.** Always use the school's official English name, found on its English website or your English transcript. Don't translate it yourself.

| Chinese | Official English name |
| --- | --- |
| 北京大学 | Peking University |
| 中国人民大学 | Renmin University of China |
| 上海交通大学 | Shanghai Jiao Tong University |
| 中山大学 | Sun Yat-sen University |

**Companies.** Use the company's own English name where it has one (字节跳动 → ByteDance; 美团 → Meituan). US recruiters may not know Chinese companies, so add a short description the first time:

> **Product Intern**, Meituan (food-delivery and local-services platform, 700M+ users) · Beijing · Jun 2023 – Aug 2023

For smaller companies, describe the business: "Xinghe Tech (B2B logistics software startup, 50 employees)".

## Step 3: Translate job titles by function, not by word

Chinese titles often don't map one to one. Translate what the job was, using the title a US company would give it.

| Chinese | Literal | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 数据分析实习生 | Data analysis intern | Data Analyst Intern |
| 产品运营 | Product operation | Product Operations Specialist, or Growth Associate if the work was growth |
| 新媒体运营 | New media operation | Social Media Marketing Intern |
| 管培生 | Management trainee | Management Trainee (standard in the US too) |
| 学生会主席 | Student union chairman | President, Student Union |
| 班长 | Monitor | Class Representative |
| 科研助理 | Scientific research assistant | Research Assistant |

When in doubt, search LinkedIn for people doing the same work at US companies and use their title.

## Step 4: Explain awards and rankings

A US recruiter doesn't know what 国家奖学金 means or how hard it is to win. Translate the name, then add the selectivity.

> **National Scholarship**, Ministry of Education of China (awarded to top 0.2% of undergraduates nationwide), 2022

Do the same for 三好学生 (Merit Student), 优秀毕业生 (Outstanding Graduate) and competition prizes, using the actual selection rate from your school or the competition rules, such as "top 8% of 300 students". Rankings US readers understand at a glance are the most persuasive: "1st of 120 teams", "top 3%".

## Step 5: Convert the GPA and dates

**GPA.** Many Chinese universities use a 100-point scale. A US reader seeing "GPA: 86" may assume a mistake. Either:

- write the score with its scale: "Average score: 86/100 (rank 12/180)", or
- use a 4.0-scale GPA from an official evaluation such as WES, or from your university if it issues one.

Don't run your own conversion and present it as official.

**Dates.** Replace "2022.09–2023.06" with "Sep 2022 – Jun 2023". US parsing software reads that format reliably.

## Step 6: Rewrite the bullets, don't translate them

This step matters most. Chinese resumes often list duties: 负责…, 参与…, 协助… Translated directly, they become "Responsible for…", "Participated in…", "Assisted with…", the weakest openings in English.

Rewrite each bullet to answer: **what did you do, how, and what changed?**

Chinese:

> 负责公司公众号的日常运营，参与活动策划。

Literal translation:

> Responsible for the daily operation of the company's WeChat official account and participated in event planning.

Rewritten for a US resume:

> Grew the company's WeChat account from 8K to 25K followers in 6 months by launching a weekly case-study series; co-planned 3 product launch events with 400+ attendees.

The rewrite is longer but says much more. It also explains WeChat in passing, so a US reader knows it's a major platform.

Strong opening verbs: built, launched, analyzed, designed, led, grew, reduced, automated, negotiated, wrote.

## Step 7: Remove every Chinese character

Some applicant tracking systems turn Chinese characters into boxes or question marks, which can garble the line around them. After translating, search the document for any leftover characters, including in school names, file names and email signatures.

You can keep a note of fluency in the Skills section: "Languages: Mandarin (native), English (fluent)." For US employers with China-facing teams, that is a real asset.

## Step 8: Check the result

Read the English version as a US recruiter would, in 10 seconds. Can you tell what you studied, what your best two experiences were, and what you achieved? If not, cut more.
