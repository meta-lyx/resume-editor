A resume that works in Beijing, Mumbai or Seoul can quietly fail in the US. Not because your experience is weaker, but because the format breaks rules American recruiters take for granted. A photo, a birth date or a two-page list of every class you took can get a strong candidate passed over in seconds.

This guide covers what to change. It takes about an hour, and you only do it once.

## Remove: personal details US employers avoid

US anti-discrimination law bars employers from making hiring decisions based on age, gender, race, religion, national origin, marital status and several other traits. Because of that, many recruiters would rather not see this information at all. Some will set a resume aside rather than risk a complaint.

Delete all of these:

| Common at home | On a US resume |
| --- | --- |
| Photo | Remove |
| Date of birth or age | Remove |
| Gender | Remove |
| Marital status, children | Remove |
| Nationality, ethnicity, hometown | Remove |
| Political affiliation (e.g. party or youth league membership) | Remove |
| Height, weight, health | Remove |
| ID or passport number | Remove, never share this on a resume |
| Full home address | City and state only, e.g. "New York, NY" |

You do not need to hide that you are international. Your schools and work history will show it, and that is fine. The point is to remove details that are irrelevant to the job.

## Change: the header

A US header is short: name, city and state, phone, email, LinkedIn.

> **Wei (David) Zhang**
> New York, NY · (646) 555-0142 · wei.zhang@nyu.edu · linkedin.com/in/weizhang

A few details matter:

- **Name order.** Write your given name first and family name last, as Americans do. If you use an English name, put it in parentheses or use it with your legal family name, and use the same version on LinkedIn.
- **Phone.** Use a US number. Recruiters rarely call international numbers. A free Google Voice number works if you don't have a US plan yet.
- **Email.** Use your university address or a professional Gmail. Some company filters treat unfamiliar domains such as qq.com or 163.com with suspicion.

## Change: length

For students and new graduates, **one page**. US recruiters spend seconds on a first pass, and a second page usually holds the weakest material. Two pages are fine only with several years of relevant full-time experience.

If you are over a page, cut in this order: high school, coursework lists, generic skills ("Microsoft Word", "teamwork"), and older roles unrelated to the job.

## Change: section order

The standard order for a student or new grad:

1. **Education**: degree, school, graduation month and year, GPA if 3.3/4.0 or above
2. **Experience**: internships, part-time jobs, research positions
3. **Projects**: class, personal or competition projects with real results
4. **Skills**: tools, programming languages, spoken languages
5. **Leadership & activities** (optional)

Once you have a year or more of full-time experience, move Education below Experience.

Use plain headings: "Experience", not "My Professional Journey". An [applicant tracking system](/blog/ats-friendly-resume-format) sorts your resume by these headings.

## Change: dates, GPA and names

**Dates.** Use "Jun 2023 – Aug 2023" or "06/2023 – 08/2023". Formats like "2023.06" are normal elsewhere but can confuse US parsing software.

**GPA.** US readers expect a 4.0 scale. If your school used a 100-point scale, either write it with the scale ("Average: 88/100") or use a converted GPA from a credential evaluation such as WES. Never convert it yourself and present it as official.

**School and company names.** Use the official English name of your university ("Zhejiang University", not a translation you made up). For companies a US reader may not know, add a few words of context:

> **Data Analyst Intern**, Meituan (China's largest food-delivery platform) · Beijing · Jun 2023 – Aug 2023

That one line tells a US recruiter your internship was at a company with hundreds of millions of users. Without it, the name means nothing to them.

For a full walkthrough of translating names, titles and awards, see [how to translate a Chinese resume into English](/blog/translate-chinese-resume-to-english).

## Rewrite: bullets as results, not duties

Many resume traditions describe responsibilities. US resumes describe results. Each bullet should start with a strong verb and, wherever possible, end with a number.

Before:

> Responsible for data analysis of user orders.

After:

> Analyzed 50M+ order records in SQL to find drop-off points in checkout; the fix lifted conversion 4%.

Aim for three to five bullets per role and a number in at least half of them. Numbers can be scale (users, records, budget), change (% faster, hours saved) or rank (top 5 of 200 teams).

Drop "I", "me" and "my". US resumes leave out the subject: "Built a dashboard", not "I built a dashboard".

## Add: what US recruiters look for

A few things matter more in the US than in many other markets:

- **A Skills section** with specific tools, written the way job postings spell them ("Power BI", "Python (pandas)").
- **Projects**, especially if you lack US work experience. A well-described class project can carry real weight. See [how to write a resume with no US experience](/blog/resume-for-international-students-no-us-experience).
- **Keywords from each job posting.** Recruiters search their ATS for the exact skills in the job ad. Run your resume through a [free keyword matcher](/tools/resume-keyword-matcher) before each application.
- **Work authorization**, optionally. Whether to mention F-1 or OPT status is covered in [how to handle work authorization on your resume](/blog/f1-opt-resume-work-authorization).

## A quick checklist

Before you send your resume to a US employer:

- [ ] No photo, age, birth date, gender, marital status or nationality
- [ ] Given name first; US phone; professional email; LinkedIn URL
- [ ] One page (students and new grads)
- [ ] Plain section headings in the standard order
- [ ] US-style dates; GPA with its scale
- [ ] Official English names for schools; context for unfamiliar companies
- [ ] Bullets start with verbs; at least half include a number
- [ ] Keywords from the job posting, where they are true
- [ ] Saved as a text-based PDF, not a scan or image
